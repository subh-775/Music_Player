# Your Library

Everything you have kept: liked songs, playlists, albums, followed artists and downloads. It is the third tab in the bottom bar.

## Filters and pinning

Along the top: **All · Playlists · Albums · Artists**.

Long-press any row to **pin** it. Pinned rows sort first, and pinning keys off the row's *kind* — so a playlist and an album that happen to share a name pin independently.

## What lives here

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Liked Songs** | Everything you tapped ♥ on, newest first |
| **Downloads** | What is on the phone. Plays with no connection |
| **Your playlists** | Made by hand, or imported from Spotify |
| **Albums** | Opened from a song's album, or from Home |
| **Artists** | The ones you followed from an artist page |

## Playlists

Create one from the Library tab, or from **+** in the player and **Add to playlist** in a song's **⋮** menu — creating and adding in one step.

Inside a playlist you can play, shuffle, reorder, rename, remove songs and delete the whole thing. Deleting a playlist does not delete the downloads of songs that were in it; those are files, and they stay files.

## Liking

The ♥ appears on song rows and as a labelled row in the **⋮** menu. In the player and the mini player the same job belongs to **+**, which fills in once the song is saved. A liked song is added to Liked Songs and nothing else — it is not a download, and it does not make the song available offline.

Liking is the cheap gesture and downloading is the expensive one, and keeping them separate is why you can like freely.

## The green highlight

When something is playing, the collection it was **launched from** shows a green marker.

Launched from, not "contains". A song is typically in Liked Songs *and* a playlist *and* Downloads at once, so highlighting everything that contains it lit up three rows and meant nothing. The marker follows where playback actually started, which is the question you were asking.

## Followed artists

Following an artist from their page adds them here. The row opens their profile — top tracks, albums, and a follow toggle — rather than a flat tracklist, because an artist is not a playlist.

## Downloads in the library

The Downloads collection is built by scanning the folder, not by trusting a list. If you delete a file with a file manager, it disappears from here on the next scan. If you add one by hand, it shows up.

That means the library reflects the disk rather than a database that can disagree with it. See **[Downloads & Offline](/guide/downloads)**.
