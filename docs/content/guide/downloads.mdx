# Downloads & Offline

A download saves a **real audio file** to a folder you choose, with the cover and song details inside it. It is not locked to the app.

## Downloading

Tap **⭳** in the player, or long-press a row and choose **Download**.

- The first time, Android asks for storage permission.
- Progress shows on the Downloads screen.
- The button turns into a **green tick** when the file is saved.
- Downloaded songs show an **Offline** label everywhere.

## Where files go

**Settings → Download location** shows the folder and lets you change it. **Open in Files** opens it in your file manager.

The files are normal audio files. Other music apps can play them, you can copy them to a computer, and **they stay if you uninstall Relaxify**.

<Danger title="What uninstalling deletes">
  Your downloads stay. Your **playlists, likes and history are deleted**, because Android removes app data on uninstall. To update, install the new version over the old one. The in-app updater does this for you.
</Danger>

## Streaming saves nothing

Playing a song does not keep a copy. If you want a song on your phone, download it. That way you always know what is saved.

## How the app spots a download

By **title and artist**, read from the file's tags.

- Renaming the file is fine.
- Changing its **title or artist tag** breaks the match, and the song shows as not downloaded.
- Two different recordings with the same title and artist count as one.

<Note>
  If a song stops showing as downloaded, its tags changed. Download it again. The folder is read again each time you open the Library tab.
</Note>

## Deleting

Delete from the Downloads screen. This removes **the file**, not just the list entry.

Deleting a download does not unlike the song or remove it from playlists.

## With no connection

- **Downloads play normally.**
- Search shows nothing, because it needs the three sources.
- Playlists still open. Downloaded songs in them play, and the rest are skipped with a note.

## Covers

The cover is saved inside the file. The app also remembers the cover it saw, so a file without one still shows the right picture.
