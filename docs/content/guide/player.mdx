# The Player

This is where the app stops feeling like a search box. Tap the mini player to open it; drag it down to put it away.

## Reading the screen

| Where | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Top strip** | The album you are inside of, or “Now playing”. The chevron minimises — so does dragging anywhere on this strip |
| **Artwork** | Swipe it sideways to change song, double-tap either edge to seek |
| **Title** | The song, at full width |
| **Artist** | Its own line. Tapping it opens that artist; where several are credited, it asks which one first |
| **Badges** | Which source is streaming and at what bitrate. Both can be switched off under Settings → Appearance |
| **⭳ +** | Download on the left, save on the right. The download glyph becomes a green tick once the file is on the phone. **+** saves to Liked Songs on the first press and fills in; pressing it again opens the full list of playlists, and holding it goes straight there |
| **Output** | Under the buttons, in green, when something other than the phone speaker is connected |
| **Capsule** | Song or lyrics, in the middle of the timestamp row. Tapping it anywhere flips to the other one. It goes dim when the track genuinely has no lyrics |
| **Bottom row** | A **timer** on the left and the **queue** on the right. The timer turns green while a sleep timer is running |

## The seek bar

Drag to scrub, tap to jump. The played portion fills ahead of the handle, so position reads at a glance.

<SeekDemo />

Two things are true of it that are not obvious:

- **The seek happens on release**, not continuously. One range request, not one per pixel — which is what keeps scrubbing smooth on a slow connection.
- **The touch target is 44 pixels tall for a 4-pixel line.** A thin bar is easy to look at and impossible to hit. A clearly *vertical* drag that starts on the bar still falls through to minimising the player, so the two gestures do not fight.

## Transport

**Shuffle** is a real toggle, not a re-roll. Turning it on shuffles everything still to come; turning it off puts the original order back. Pressing it repeatedly does not keep re-shuffling — that behaviour reads as "still on" and leaves songs playing in an order you never chose.

**Previous** restarts the current song first. It only jumps to the song before when you are already within the first three seconds. One stray tap cannot lose your place.

**Repeat** is two-state: off, or repeat this song. It loops the current track when it ends — dominating shuffle and autoplay, which is what "replay" means — while the next button still moves on manually.

## Song and lyrics

The capsule in the middle of the timestamp row switches the artwork for the lyrics. **Tapping anywhere on it flips to the other one** — it is a two-state switch, so there is only ever one thing a tap can mean, and half of it is never a dead target.

It goes **dim** when this song genuinely has none, which is common for SoundCloud and YouTube uploads. That is not a failed fetch — the app checked and there is nothing there. Tapping it says so rather than opening a pane that only ever reads "nothing here". See [Lyrics](/guide/lyrics).

## The bottom row

Two glyphs sit under the transport controls: a **timer** on the left, the **queue** on the right.

**Tap the queue glyph, or pull up anywhere along the row.** The pull opens the sheet as the drag is recognised rather than when you let go, so it is already on its way up under your finger; push back down before releasing and it goes away again. Tapping only counts on the glyph itself — the rest of the row is for the pull, so a tap meant for the pane switch cannot open the queue by accident.

The pull is upward-only on purpose: a downward drag there has to keep falling through to the dismiss, or the strip where your thumb naturally rests becomes a dead zone for minimising the player.

**The timer** arms a sleep timer — 15, 30, 45 or 60 minutes, or the end of this track. It goes green while one is running, which is the only way to know without opening it. See [Settings](/reference/settings#sleep-timer).

## Notification and lock screen

While music plays, Relaxify shows a notification with transport controls. The same controls appear on your lock screen, and on Bluetooth devices, car head units and headsets.

- Play, pause, skip and the scrubber all work from there.
- The notification is what **keeps music alive when you leave the app** — Android requires a foreground service for background audio, and that notification is it.
- A headset's **previous** button restarts the track first, same as the in-app button.

<Note>
  Swiping the app away from Recents stops playback and removes the notification. That is intended. If music kept playing after a swipe-away on an older build, updating fixes it — see [Troubleshooting](/reference/troubleshooting).
</Note>

## Resume

The app reopens on the song and timestamp you left, **paused**, with your queue.

Paused rather than playing is deliberate. An app that starts making noise the moment you open it is an app you stop opening in public.
