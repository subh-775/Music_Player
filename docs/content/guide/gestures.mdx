# Gestures

The app is built to be driven one-handed. Nothing below is the only way to do the thing — there is always a button — but these are the fast ways, and they are what the app was designed around.

## Everywhere else

| Gesture | Where | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Swipe sideways** | Mini player | Next or previous song. The row moves with your finger; let go short and it springs back |
| **Tap** | Mini player | Opens the full player |
| **Drag up** | Mini player | Opens the full player *under your finger* — see below |
| **Long-press** | Any song row | Its actions: play next, add to queue, playlist, artist, album, download |
| **Drag down** | Any sheet | Dismisses it. Where the sheet holds a list, the list scrolls to its top first, then the sheet follows your finger. Tapping the dimmed area behind it also closes it |
| **Drag** | Equalizer band | Sets that frequency, snapping to whole decibels |
| **Drag or tap** | Crossfade bar | Sets the overlap, snapping to whole seconds |
| **Press and hold, then drag** | Queue row handle | Reorders it |
| **Back** | Anywhere | Closes what is actually on top, one layer at a time |

## The player becomes the bar

Dragging the mini player up does not slide a new screen over the old one, and dragging the full player down does not slide it away. The panel and the bar are the same surface at two sizes.

Pull down on the big player and its **boundary** travels to the bar's: the panel narrows, shortens and rounds its corners until it is exactly the bar's rectangle, in the bar's place. The artwork rides along on top, shrinking to the size of the small cover and landing precisely where it sits. The words and the controls clear out early, so what you are watching is a picture in a shrinking frame rather than a screen being dismantled.

Pull up on the mini player and the same thing runs backwards, tracking your finger the whole way. Let go halfway and it returns; keep going and the panel is already open.

Nothing fades out from full size, which is the point — a large surface disappearing at the end of a gesture reads as a flash, whereas one that is already bar-sized by the time it goes reads as an arrival. A tap and the ⌄ button run the same transition at the same speed.

<Note>
  Both covers are the same image at the same size on purpose, which is why the swap never flickers — the picture is already decoded before the panel opens. The transition is deliberately unhurried: fast enough not to wait for, slow enough to actually see.
</Note>

## How two gestures share the artwork

The artwork carries a swipe *and* a double-tap, and they never fight, because the axis is decided **before either has taken a frame**:

- A clearly horizontal movement is a **swipe**, claimed as soon as it is unambiguous.
- A clearly vertical movement falls through to the **dismiss**.
- A touch that does not move is a **tap**, and the double-tap zones get it.

The mini player splits the same way, at the same threshold on both axes: fourteen pixels sideways is a skip, fourteen pixels up is an open, and a diagonal drag resolves to whichever way it is actually leaning.

A swipe also needs a real push before it commits. Let go early and the cover springs back and nothing changes — that threshold is what stops a sloppy double-tap becoming an accidental skip.

<Tip title="Stacking seeks">
  Double-tap the same side repeatedly and the jumps stack: 10 → 20 → 30 seconds. Once the chain has started, single taps keep counting, so you do not have to keep double-tapping.
</Tip>

## Back

Back walks the same stack the on-screen arrows do — innermost surface first, one layer per press.

Queue sheet → player → sheet → overlay → tab → Home. On Home with nothing open, one press warns and a second within two seconds exits, so a stray press cannot kill the music by accident.

## About that left edge

Android reserves roughly the outer 20–24 density pixels of each side of the screen for its own back gesture, and it takes those touches **before any app sees them**.

Relaxify formally asks the system for a 28-pixel strip on the left edge of Home. That is why the drawer swipe works at all, and why it is a narrow strip rather than the whole screen.

<Note>
  If your phone is set to three-button navigation the conflict disappears entirely. Either way the hamburger in the Home header always opens the drawer.
</Note>

## Why these are gestures and not buttons

Every one of them replaces a target you would otherwise have to aim at with your thumb, near the top of a screen that is now six inches tall.

The artwork is the biggest target in the app. Making it *do* something — three things, in fact — is worth more than any number of small icons arranged around it.
