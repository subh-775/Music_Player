# Data & storage

You need none of this to use the app. It is here because "where does my stuff go" deserves a real answer rather than a promise.

## Everything happens on the phone

Searching, ranking results, resolving a stream, downloading — all of it runs on your handset. There is no service of this project's in between, which is why there is no account and nothing to sign in to. It is also why a search is only as quick as your connection to the catalogues themselves, and why a source having a bad day is a source having a bad day.

The engine that does this work is a small server running **inside the app, on your phone only**. It is not reachable from your network. It is guarded by a password the app generates fresh on every launch and shares with nothing, because on Android "only on this device" is not by itself a boundary — other apps on the same phone can reach it, and so, in a limited way, can a web page open in your browser.

**v1.2.1** narrows the browser case. The engine used to let any web page *read* its replies, which meant a site you visited could ask "are you there?" and be told. It no longer sends the permission that made those replies readable, so a page gets nothing back.

To be exact about what that does and does not fix: a determined page can still infer the app is installed from whether the request succeeds at all, and closing that completely is a change still to come. Nothing of yours was reachable in either case — the password guards every route that touches your library, and that has always held. This is about not confirming the app exists to sites with no business knowing.

## What is stored, and where

| What | Where |
| --- | --- |
| Playlists, likes, history, settings | App storage on the device |
| Downloaded songs | Your own music folder, outside app storage |
| Lyrics, artwork, temporary files | Cache, cleared by **Settings → Clear cache** |

Uninstalling the app removes the first row. It does not touch the second — downloads live in a folder you chose, and they survive both an uninstall and a reinstall. See [Downloads](/guide/downloads).

## Audio effects

The equalizer and volume normalization are Android's own effects, attached to the audio session that is playing right now.

That is why they need something playing to attach to, why your device's real band count shows up in the hint, and why an effect is only ever created when it is going to be used — inserting one makes the audio system re-route the mix, which is audible.

## Playback survives the app

Android requires a **foreground service** for background audio, and that service is what the playback notification represents. It is not decoration: without the notification there is no background playback, which is why the app asks for notification permission the first time you open it.

Swiping the app away from Recents stops playback and removes the notification. That is intended.
